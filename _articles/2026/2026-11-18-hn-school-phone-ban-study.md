## 原文概要

2026 年 11 月 17 日，《纽约时报》发了一篇带数字的研究报道：校园手机禁令开始见效。报道署名 Claire Cain Miller，研究由哈佛大学教育政策研究中心（Center for Education Policy Research）于当周发布，作者之一是哈佛教育研究生院教授 Thomas Kane。

样本规模是这项研究最硬的底气。2021 到 2026 年间，330 万名学生、3 至 10 年级、4600 所美国学校；研究用 MAP 测评（从秋季到春季追踪个人成就）比较了「严格禁手机」的学校与条件相似但没有禁令的学校。结果是数学和阅读成绩都出现提升，效果相当于每个学生一学年多上了一到两周课；每年的增幅，大致补回了疫情前普通学生平均掉分的一半。

有两个细节让研究者自己意外。其一是所有水平的学生都受益——他们原本预期低分学生受益最多，密歇根大学教授 Brian A. Jacob 说「知道没有差别，这真的既有意思又有用」。其二是阅读方向的效果更明显：分数下滑的学生变少了，研究者认为阅读需要持续专注，屏幕又会挤占本该用来阅读的时间。相对宽松的政策（只要求上课别碰手机）同样显示出提升，但研究者对这部分结果信心较低，因为两类学校在其他方面也不可比。

争议当然还在。这项研究之前有两个结论不一致的前作：2025 年佛罗里达一个学区发现第二年有小幅提升，另一项全国性研究用学校的平均分对比，没找到统计显著的变化。Jacob 认为新研究与他们团队的结果「相当类似」，差别在于新研究追踪的是个体在同一份测评上的成长，样本覆盖全国并延伸到 2025—26 学年。他同时提醒别对不大的提升过于乐观：「如果它们年复一年地累积起来，那可能真的非常重要、真的有意义。」

与此呼应的是另一份研究。2026 年 10 月 28 日，它出现在 JAMA Network Open 上：冰岛全国性「Youth in Iceland」学校调查的三轮数据（2016 年 2 月、2018 年 2 月、2023 年 11 月），覆盖 70 所学校、23,938 个学生观测，年龄 13 至 15 岁，用双重差分比较 2018 到 2023 年间实施禁令的 22 所学校与未实施的学校。结论是抑郁症状、焦虑、敌意、心理幸福感四项指标都没有出现有意义的变化，作者写道「单独实施的校园手机禁令，在总体层面可能不足以改善青少年心理健康」。两篇研究一前一后：分数在涨，心理健康没动。

## 讨论焦点

### 一、共识比研究先到：手机当年是怎么进教室的

评论区第一条就把结论摆出来了，研究反而排在后面：

> "Amazing to me they were ever allowed in the first place." — Eddy_Viscosity2 [c:50530530]
> （译文：真难以想象当初居然是允许的。）

真正的问题因此不是「该不该禁」，而是「为什么一开始没禁得住」。一位当老师的朋友给出的观察是：

> "A friend who is a teacher said if you try to confiscate them you get parents showing up complaining it’s a safety issue." — dan_grec [c:50530654]
> （译文：我有个当老师的朋友说，你一旦没收手机，家长就会找上门投诉，说是安全问题。）

把这条链条讲得最完整的是这条回复——不是规则没有写，而是没人执行得了：

> "They never were &quot;allowed&quot; but teachers don&#x27;t have any actual power to confiscate the phone and then if some teacher did confiscate the phone parents would step in making a fuss about children rights yada yada.<p>So it ended up teachers not enforcing it and kids not being afraid because no one could do anything.<p>People are quite quick with not caring about rules they are not going to be punished or ostracised for..." — ozim [c:50531125]
> （译文：手机从来没有被「允许」过，但老师根本没有权力没收。真要是没收，家长就会跳出来拿孩子的权利说事闹起来。结果就是老师不执行，学生也不怕，因为谁也拿他们没办法。人对那些不会被惩罚、也不会被孤立的规则，总是很自然地不当回事。）

于是评论区开始算这批家长的比例，以及公立学校在法律上退到什么程度：

> "To be clear it&#x27;s about 2% of parents that do this, if that. And they are consistently horrible parents - shocker. The worst part in modern times with social media and the like is that people started caring what the 2% think for fear they&#x27;ll got rant on social media or whatever. Not to say we should be discriminatory or whatever, but seriously - the unambiguously correct answer to this is &#x27;We&#x27;re sorry to hear you feel that way, and wish you the best of luck finding a new school for your child.&#x27;" — somenameforme [c:50530693]
> （译文：说清楚点，这种家长占 2%，最多就 2%，而且一贯是最差劲的那种家长。现代最大的问题是社交媒体让人开始在乎那 2% 的声音，怕他们在网上发疯写小作文。当然不是说该歧视他们，但说真的，唯一正确的回应只能是：「很抱歉听到您这么说，也祝您早日为孩子找到合适的学校。」）

> "There are two problems. One is that we&#x27;ve let that 2% hold <i>everything</i> hostage.<p>Another is that a public school is legally required to offer an education. All children are legally entitled to an education. If you&#x27;re private, you can tell them to pound sand." — Mezzie [c:50531269]
> （译文：这里有两个问题。一个是我们让那 2% 挟持了所有人。另一个是公立学校在法律上有义务提供教育——所有孩子都有受教育的法定权利。如果是私立学校，你完全可以让他们靠边站。）

禁令要落地，最后靠的是一件件硬件：储物柜、锁袋、收纳箱。有的学校储物柜不够用，只能改用需要老师逐个检查的锁袋：

> "They now have switched to lockable pouches that phones have to go in during the day and are locked during school hours (with an &#x27;unlocker&#x27; available when they leave school). This works better but requires quite a bit of staff time checking the pouches in the morning (students will do things like put calculators inside instead!) and costs money. I can see why not all schools have gone that route." — jvvw [c:50531159]
> （译文：他们现在改用可上锁的收纳袋，手机白天必须放进去，上学期间锁上（放学时有人来解锁）。这个办法效果更好，但需要不少老师的时间早上来检查（学生会把计算器塞进袋子里冒充），也要花钱。我能理解为什么不是所有学校都走这条路。）

家长自己也在用笨办法绕开问题。有位读者描述了学校买收纳箱之后的场景：12 岁的儿子手机放在父母桌上，想用要解锁，一放开完整权限使用时间就涨到每天几个小时：

> "My older son (12) has his phone on my desk, and I unlock it for him when he wants it. Tried many times to give him full access, only to find out the phone usage skyrocketed to several hours a day.<p>When they go on a school trip, he&#x27;s usually the only one without a phone and watches others glued to their screens, even when they have free time to run in a park.<p>Now, finally, the school purchased a box where kids lock their phones in at the start of the day and recover them at the end. They seem to enjoy it as well." — vitro [c:50530756]
> （译文：我大儿子 12 岁，手机放在我桌上，他想用的时候我替他解锁。我试过很多次把完整权限给他，最后发现使用时间直接涨到每天好几个小时。学校郊游时，他通常是唯一没带手机的，看着别人盯着屏幕，哪怕是在公园里没事干的时候。现在学校终于买了一个箱子，学生早上把手机锁进去，放学再拿回来。他们似乎也挺喜欢。）

另一种思路是各自在家给孩子做限制，但立刻被指出这会制造新的问题：

> "Parents can do that (and some do), but that just turns your kid into a social pariah.<p>Kids who love school phone bans love it precisely because it affects all." — cedilla [c:50531843]
> （译文：家长也能这么做（有些人确实这么做了），但那只会让孩子变成社交上的异类。喜欢校园禁手机的孩子，恰恰因为它是对所有人一视同仁的。）

### 二、家长的安全诉求：Columbine 之后的一次交易

评论区把矛头指向了一个具体的历史节点。手机进教室这一步，被认为不是出于教学考虑，而是出于对校园枪击的无力感：

> "Phones in class seemed to be the result of the inability to deal with school shootings." — newsclues [c:50530856]
> （译文：当年教室里允许手机，感觉上是因为整个社会没法处理校园枪击这件事。）

> "Parents insisted that they needed to be in contact with their kids. Not being able to text the kids was a safety issue and strong inconvenience." — Telemakhos [c:50530878]
> （译文：是家长坚持他们必须能联系到孩子。联系不上孩子是安全问题，也确实很不方便。）

这条线上的争论很快从「家长在想什么」变成「概率是多少」。最尖锐的一句把它概括成家长想象中的风险：

> "School shootings.  That&#x27;s the safety issue parents are imagining." — cogman10 [c:50531142]
> （译文：校园枪击。这就是家长想象中的那个安全问题。）

反方则指出，一个被媒体放大的罕见事件足以绑架整个教育系统：

> "We really don&#x27;t hate the media enough. An extremely rare occurrence that gets boosted whenever one occurs has convinced every parent in America it&#x27;s happening everywhere, constantly, and thus we need to compromise the education of every student in the school to work around it.<p>Might as well leave your kid home at that point, iPad in hand. Even less &quot;&quot;risk&quot;&quot; when they were never going to learn anything at school anyway if they are allowed access to devices." — DaSHacka [c:50531253]
> （译文：我们对媒体的厌恶还不够。一个极其罕见的事件，只要发生一次就被炒上天，这已经让全美每一个家长都以为这种事到处都在、天天发生，于是我们需要牺牲全校学生的教育来为它买单。）

数据端的反驳更直接。评论者翻出了 2025 年的校园枪击统计：

> "Five people were killed just a month back. In 2025, 71 school shootings happened. That is absolutely insane." — eldaisfish [c:50531438]
> （译文：五个人上个月刚被枪杀。2025 年发生了 71 起校园枪击。这绝对疯狂得离谱。）

而另一条回复把风险排序摆了出来：

> "You&#x27;re more likely to die being driven to school than you are in a school shooting. Of those 71 - how many were targeted gang on gang violence?<p>With over 100,000 schools - school shootings, as common as they are when compared to other countries, still classify as an &quot;extremely rare event&quot;.<p>To compare it against other common concerns. You should be more worried about your child drowning than them going through a school shooting and more worried about them choking on their school lunch every day than them dying in a school shooting.<p>That parents worry about it and whether parents should be worrying about it are two different things." — Nadya [c:50531896]
> （译文：你更可能死于上下学的路上，而不是死于校园枪击。那 71 起里，有多少是有针对性的帮派火拼？在超过 10 万所学校里，校园枪击即便按全美口径也仍然属于「极其罕见的事件」。要横向比较，你该更担心孩子溺水，也该更担心孩子每天被午餐噎着，而不是校园枪击。家长担心这件事，和家长该不该担心这件事，是两回事。）

有人把这场分歧的归属地直接划清，也有评论者认为它并不专属于美国：

> "That doesn’t make sense, phones are common in EU classrooms as well and they don’t have the school shooting problem." — Insanity [c:50530900]
> （译文：这说不通，欧盟国家的教室里同样有手机啊，他们又没有校园枪击问题。）

> "France. France has shooting drills now. Fins have been forbidden by the nazis in 1941, but we didn&#x27;t do a good job recently at it. So kids are taught how to react to terrorist attacks, because it&#x27;s a useful skill to learn in school, they decided." — eastbound [c:50535305]
> （译文：法国。法国现在也有枪击演练。枪支在 1941 年被纳粹禁掉了，但我们最近没把这事做好。所以孩子们要学怎么应对恐怖袭击——学校认为这是一门值得在学校里学的技能。）

### 三、别让手机换成笔记本电脑

禁令的支持者很快遇到了反问：学校发的笔记本电脑同样可以上网、发消息，为什么不一起禁掉。

> "School-issued laptops constantly available. A phone is doing something, but there are a lot of negative factors at play." — fn-mote [c:50530632]
> （译文：学校发的笔记本电脑才是随时可用的。手机确实在做点什么，但负面因素还有很多。）

> "It is about the format. You cannot carry your laptop in your pocket and take it out every time there is the slightest bit of boredom or free time. The barrier to using phones is way, way lower." — vitro [c:50530686]
> （译文：关键在形式。你没法把笔记本装在口袋里，一有点无聊或空闲就掏出来。使用手机的门槛低太多了。）

教育背景的评论者把话说得更重，并且给出了政策建议：

> "Regardless of format, there is plenty of evidence which at minimum strongly suggests that school sized laptops are one of the leading drivers of the modern literacy crisis. As an educator I can attest to this being a seriously under appreciated issue." — ageedizzle [c:50530723]
> （译文：不管是什么形式，已有大量证据至少强烈表明，学校用的那种尺寸的笔记本电脑是当代读写能力危机的主要推手之一。作为教育工作者我可以作证，这个问题被严重低估了。）

> "Some are pushing back on the laptops too. Kids seem to do better with writing without them. I think we should go back to having computer labs with dedicated time to learn how to use them and to use them for specific things. No more screens in classrooms and no more &quot;they&#x27;re digital natives&quot; thinking.<p>The push back to that is that a lot of assignments have been moved to be digital, which saves teachers a lot of time with grading." — goda90 [c:50530814]
> （译文：现在也有人开始反对笔记本电脑了。孩子们不用电脑时写字似乎表现更好。我觉得应该回到那种有专门机房的做法，划出专门时间学怎么用电脑、用来做具体的事。教室里不要再有屏幕，也不要再有「他们是数字原住民」那套想法。反对意见是：很多作业已经数字化了，这能帮老师省下大量批改时间。）

反对者则把数字化的代价一项项摊开：

> "With the digital assignments:<p>-I can&#x27;t inspect my kid&#x27;s homework once it is done.<p>-There is no &quot;show your work&quot; so the teacher can&#x27;t find the flaw in how a kid is executing an algorithm in math, for example, the steps in long division.<p>-There is no artifact for the kid to review once it&#x27;s graded, to understand where they went wrong.<p>-There is no artifact for them to study to prep for a test.<p>-My refrigerator door is bare.<p>Downvoting doesn&#x27;t make this untrue; what a bunch of technofascists here." — buellerbueller [c:50531109]
> （译文：作业数字化之后：我没法检查孩子做完的作业；没有「展示你的解题过程」，老师看不出孩子做长除法时哪一步错了；批完之后没有留痕，孩子没法复盘自己错在哪；也没有留痕可以拿来备考。我的冰箱门上贴的东西全没了。投票不改变这些事实。）

### 四、真正的战场在校外：晚上回家之后

讨论的焦点接着从教室挪到了工位。有评论者承认自己跑题了，但给出了这次跑题最有价值的观察：

> "A little off-topic for schools, but honestly, I was always shocked by the amount of lost productivity to phones in the white-collar workplace. I worked in a cube farm, and walking around nearly everyone at their desk was looking at their phone at any given time. I can guarantee you that, most of the time, what they were looking at wasn&#x27;t work related, or at best maybe it was related in a peripheral way (stock prices, industry gossip, etc.)<p>The &quot;attention tax&quot; that is paid to a handful of firms in Silicon Valley is really unbelievable." — GlibMonkeyDeath [c:50531155]
> （译文：稍微偏题，但我一直对白领工作场所里手机造成的生产力损失感到震惊。我在格子间工作过，走一圈几乎每个人的工位上都在看手机。我敢保证，大部分时候他们看的和工作无关，或者最多只是沾边（股价、行业八卦之类的）。硅谷那少数几家公司收走的「注意力税」，真的难以置信。）

而且损失的不只是效率：

> "It&#x27;s not just productivity. It alters the workplace socially, typically in negative ways. Rather than getting to know one&#x27;s colleagues and accept them for who they are, animosity flourishes over trivial things. (I&#x27;m not claiming that workplaces were anywhere near perfect before cell phones, you still had jerks and gossiping cliques. But a lot of the time I&#x27;m like, &quot;pull your nose away from the phone and get to know them, you may actually like them.)<p>Granted, I have worked in places where the only convenient social contact you have is with people within a 1 km radius. So you either got to know the people you worked with, isolated yourself, were paying hefty long-distance bills, or were burning your paycheques to fly to the nearest city every weekend." — II2II [c:50532355]
> （译文：这不只是效率问题，它还会改变职场的社交形态，通常是往坏的方向变。你不再去了解同事、接纳他们本来的样子，反而因为琐碎的事结怨。我不是说手机之前的职场有多完美——一样有混蛋和小圈子八卦。但很多时候我会想，「把手从手机上拿开，去认识他们，你可能真的会喜欢他们。」）

有家长干脆从设备上做减法：

> "We just don&#x27;t give our daughter (11) a phone.  She has a smart watch that can text us and a few other whitelisted phone numbers (or call them).  I don&#x27;t think middle school kids need an actual phone." — aetherson [c:50531674]
> （译文：我们干脆不给我女儿（11 岁）手机。她有一块智能手表，可以给我们和其他几个白名单号码发短信、打电话。我不认为初中生真的需要一部手机。）

关联帖里的读者把同一件事说得更冷：校园那几小时本来也拦不住什么。

> "Ok, but how about student grades?  I wouldn&#x27;t have expected any impact on mental health resulting from a school ban considering the exposure they get every other hour of the day." — alienbaby [c:50299047] [thread 2]
> （译文：那学生成绩呢？校园禁令会造成心理健康上的影响，我本来就没这么指望——反正他们在一天里其余每个小时都在被屏幕暴露。）

> "The bans are very lackadaisically enforced, and this is in a state where the ban is actually mandated by law (Missouri). My daughter says in high school kids regularly have phones out in class. And a ban even if enforced would not keep kids off these harmful platforms for the many other hours in the day.<p>Why anyone would expect phone bans to improve mental health is beyond me when the majority of phone use is not happening at school.<p>Improve grades, improve classroom behavior, maybe. What about those?" — jswelker [c:50299249] [thread 2]
> （译文：禁令执行得很马虎，而且这还是在一个法律强制禁手机（密苏里州）的州。我女儿说高中里孩子们上课照样把手机拿出来。就算禁令真被执行，也拦不住孩子们在一天里的其他十几个小时继续泡在这些平台上。当大部分手机使用根本不在学校发生时，我实在不明白为什么会有人期待校园禁令能改善心理健康。也许能改善成绩、改善课堂纪律，也许吧。那这些呢？）

### 五、「让孩子学会管理科技」：同一句话的两种读法

家长的反对意见里流传最广的一句是「我的孩子需要学会管理这项技术」。评论区为这句话吵成了两派。

一派认为这句话听起来合理，实际上危险：

> "You&#x27;d be surprised how some parents advocate using phones because &quot;My kids need to learn how to manage the technology&quot;. Well, kids don&#x27;t, since the technology exploits our primal drives. You don&#x27;t give heroin to kids so they know how to manage it." — vitro [c:50530721]
> （译文：你会惊讶有多少家长主张用手机，理由是「我的孩子需要学会管理这项技术」。可孩子不需要，因为这项技术利用的是人的原始冲动。你不会为了让孩子学会管理，就把海洛因交给孩子。）

另一派承认其中的道理，但指出正确的解法不是放开：

> "&gt; My kids need to learn how to manage the technology<p>there is some value about this. Unlike heroin, at some point the kids will have to use technology and learn to manage it.<p>But there must be better ways than unfettered access during school hours." — gpderetta [c:50530793]
> （译文：这句话里有一部分是对的。和海洛因不同，孩子终究得使用技术、得学会管理它。但一定有比在上课时间完全放开访问更好的办法。）

> "What is there to learn? Phones are locked down. You can&#x27;t learn much about technology with a locked down device." — OroPla [c:50532557]
> （译文：有什么可学的？手机是被锁住的。你没法在一台被锁住的设备上学到多少关于技术的东西。）

还有人指出这句辩解在逻辑上站不住：

> "&gt; My kids need to learn how to manage the technology&quot;<p>which is a bonkers excuse because there isnt a single other technology we require 24&#x2F;7 access to. Its more likely that some parents expect 24&#x2F;7 access to their kids." — FranzFerdiNaN [c:50530912]
> （译文：这是个离谱的借口，因为世上没有哪一项技术是我们要求 24/7 随叫随到的。更可能的情况是，某些家长想要的是对孩子的 24/7 随叫随到。）

主张给孩子上一门成瘾课的建议，把争论推向了更长的时间尺度：

> "I agree on heroin, but I think we should give all teens a class on recognizing and managing addiction. Take them through a few cycles of getting on and off caffeine, plus various digital addictions. Alongside traditional education on recreational drugs, prescription drugs, nicotine, alcohol, gambling, and the nature of addiction. And of course spotting addictions in others and how to help them.<p>Given the widespread commerical exploitation of engineered addictions, I think it&#x27;s vital knowledge to have a good life." — wpietri [c:50530775]
> （译文：海洛因那点我同意，但我觉得应该给所有青少年开一门课，教他们识别和应对成瘾：走几轮咖啡因的上瘾-戒断，再加上各种数字成瘾，和传统教育里的娱乐性药物、处方药、尼古丁、酒精、赌博，以及成瘾本身。还要包括识别身边人的成瘾，以及怎么帮他们。考虑到商业化、成规模地被设计出来的成瘾机制，这些是很要紧的生存知识。）

同一位评论者给了一个更乐观的类比——德国人处理酒精的方式：

> "actually that&#x27;s one plausible reason why Germans handle alcohol so much better than Australians and Americans. They get access to it as teenagers supervised by parents and don&#x27;t go crazy, so they don&#x27;t associate alcohol with going crazy." — cowlevel [c:50530760]
> （译文：其实这能解释为什么德国人处理酒精比澳大利亚人和美国人强得多：他们在还是青少年的时候就能在父母监督下接触酒精，所以不会失控，也就不会把「喝到失控」和酒精本身绑在一起。）

最后是那句关于周期的判断，它同时也是这次讨论里少见的、指向未来的推演：

> "You don&#x27;t know what you have until you don&#x27;t have it. This generation knows what it&#x27;s like with phones because they experienced school with phones. The next generation who grows up with the phone ban will probably think it&#x27;s pretty stupid, because they don&#x27;t know what it was like without it. Then they&#x27;ll repeal it again and go through the same cycle." — cowlevel [c:50530751]
> （译文：直到你失去过，才知道自己曾拥有什么。这一代人知道有手机在学校的日子是什么滋味。下一批在禁令下长大的孩子，大概会觉得这很蠢，因为他们不知道没有手机是什么样。然后他们会再废除一次，走一遍同样的循环。）

### 六、第二份研究：分数之外的另一半

关联帖那边，读者对着 JAMA 那篇论文的表格逐行读，提出的问题比结论更致命。

> "Am I reading Table 1 correctly? It seems to indicate screen time (social media, gaming, etc) is the same between both groups (phones allowed and phones banned). Is that right?<p>If so, it&#x27;s no wonder they see no difference. They didn&#x27;t see a change in the presumed underlying cause." — nsagent [c:50299175] [thread 2]
> （译文：我读对表 1 了吗？看起来两组（允许带手机和禁手机）的屏幕时间（社交媒体、游戏等）是一样的，是这样吗？如果是，那就难怪他们看不到差异了——他们预设的那个原因本身并没有改变。）

> "The narrative says that phone use was already banned during classroom instruction in many of the schools that later instituted school-wide bans.  The impact on students would&#x27;ve been minimal because realistically the only time they would&#x27;ve been able to use phones beforehand was passing between classes or at lunch&#x2F;recess." — nostrademons [c:50299216] [thread 2]
> （译文：叙事是这样的：在那些后来实施全校禁令的学校里，课堂时间内本来就禁止使用手机。所以影响会很小，因为现实中学生之前能用手机的时间，也就是课间、午餐或休息时间。这对政策制定者依然有意义……）

而被翻出来的那条利益冲突披露，成了整个关联帖最尖锐的一击：

> （译文：利益冲突披露：Candice Odgers 博士披露自己是 YouTube「青少年与家庭顾问委员会」成员。就这一句够了。）

质疑研究本身的声音同样直接：

但也有人做了相反的推论，把这份「没效果」读成了对禁令的背书：

### 七、评论区里的时间旅行

话题走远之后，评论区开始各自回忆自己的学生时代，这一段几乎没有数据，却最能看出讨论参与者的年龄层。

> "It wasn&#x27;t until 24&#x2F;7 social media that it started to have such a negative impact though. My first phone I think was a Sony Ericson Z-something, and of course me and everyone else brought their phones to school so we could share mp3s over IR during recess, but during class the most distracted you ended up being was trying to type and send SMSes while not looking at the screen.<p>A far cry from what seems to be going on with schools today due to smartphones, social media and all that comes with essentially &quot;one big 24&#x2F;7 public group&quot;.<p>My guess is that a &quot;social media ban&quot; would have the same effect, but it&#x27;s a lot harder to put in place, so easier to just ban phones overall." — embedding-shape [c:50530599]

> "Maybe this is an American thing? Here in Ireland lots of kids would have had 2G &#x27;smart phones&#x27; capable of running AIM, email, etc in the mid 2000s. > "It&#x27;s generational. The parents that didn&#x27;t grow up with phones at a young age don&#x27;t understand how addictive&#x2F; isolating a phone can be.  Millennials grew up with it and understand." — LocalMaximus [c:50531099]

## 总体情绪

这帖的表面话题是两项教育研究，实际清算的是一笔二十年旧账：手机是在哪一年、以什么理由被放进教室的，代价又由谁承担。评论区几乎没有人在争论「研究结论是否可信」，绝大多数人直接在讨论执行——谁有权没收、家长凭什么闹、储物柜够不够、锁袋谁来检查。这说明在 HN 读者这里，校园禁手机早已不是需要说服对方才成立的政策，剩下的争议只有成本和边界。

分歧因此集中在两处。第一处是概率：家长要的是「随时联系得上孩子」的确定性，评论者要的是「一年 71 起校园枪击，但同时溺水与噎食的风险都更高」这样的量级，两边其实在谈两套不同的风险函数。第二处是场外：分数的提升被算在了教室里，但同一个晚上刷手机占掉的睡眠、作业时间和注意力并不在学校里，JAMA 那份「心理健康没有变化」的结果其实把这一点摆到了台面上。多数人没有被研究本身说服，他们是被那句「如果它们年复一年累积起来」说服的——慢，但可累积。

所以最有价值的结论来自评论区里那句没人当回事的观察：孩子在禁令下喜欢学校，是因为它对所有人一视同仁；一旦改成每个家庭各自用软件限制，一个孩子就变成了社交场上的异类。禁手机能不能提高分数，取决于一个储物柜和一位不怕得罪家长的校长。
