## 原文概要

Car and Driver 转述 Automotive News 的报道：日本汽车制造商协会（JAMA）会长、同时也是丰田副会长的佐藤浩二（Koji Sato）本周在东京的一场记者会上宣布，Toyota、Nissan 与 Honda 将开始统一部分零部件标准，目的是压低生产成本。首批落地的三块内容是：钢材的统一质量标准、塑料与树脂的统一处理规程，以及线束（wiring harness）的通用色标规范。

选这三块的理由报道里写得很直白：它们都是消费者基本注意不到的东西，不会削弱各家的品牌辨识度，同时又在生产成本里占相当比重，因此省下来的钱能立刻兑现。JAMA 的长期计划则是把标准化扩展到覆盖整车的更多零部件。执行层面，首轮讨论先在日本国内的零部件供应商之间展开，国际供应商随后纳入；首批标准化措施明年落地。

压力来源写在报道的最后一段：包括 Toyota、Nissan、Honda、Mazda、Subaru、Mitsubishi 与 Suzuki 在内的日本七家车厂，正一起面对中国车厂越来越激烈的竞争。佐藤浩二在记者会上的原话是：「We all share this strong sense of crisis, creating a momentum for change.」「Based upon our sense of a crisis, we decided to choose these three areas.」报道署名是 Car and Driver 副编辑 Jack Fitzgerald。

## 讨论焦点

### 一、这不是新事：标准化一直在发生，只是没人统计

第一条回复就定了调——这事早该做了。

> "Batteries, spark plugs, fuses, and indicator lamps have been standardized for a century." — kevin_thibedeau [c:50596315]
> （译文：电池、火花塞、保险丝和指示灯已经标准化了一个世纪。）

立刻有人拆台：他数过的型号两只手都数不过来，而且从没开过 1979 年之前造的车。这条反驳其实指向整场讨论最关键的裂缝——标准存在，不等于同一件东西。螺纹标准化了一百年，可灯泡、保险丝、电池端子的尺寸与安装方式照样五花八门。

> "&quot;Standardized&quot; in the same sense that screw threads are standardized. That doesn&#x27;t mean collapsing all the way down to a one-size-fits-all part." — wtallis [c:50596394]
> （译文：这里的「标准化」是像螺丝螺纹那样的标准化，不等于把零件一路压缩成一个通用型号。）

也有人提醒这已经是老传统：跨车型共用的平台早就存在，Ford 与 Chevy 在皮卡上共用 10L80/10R80 变速箱就是现成的例子。

> "This already happens.  For example, Ford and Chevy collaborated on the 10L80&#x2F;10R80 transmissions that they use on their trucks." — porkshoe [c:50596415]
> （译文：这事早就在发生了。比如 Ford 和 Chevy 合作开发了他们卡车用的 10L80/10R80 变速箱。）

支持者则指出，对燃油车来说内部机械结构几十年几乎没变，变的只是 ECU 这类电子集成；标准化对消费者是好事——造得更便宜，修起来也更便宜。

> "Is that a problem? It&#x27;s a car, an ICE car&#x27;s internals have been pretty much the same for decades. Except obviously the integration of electronics like the ECU.<p>Standardization of parts is good for the consumers, cheaper to build and cheaper to maintain." — AIShill101 [c:50597075]
> （译文：这算什么问题？车就是车，燃油车的内部机械结构几十年来基本没变，显然变的是 ECU 这类电子件的集成方式。<br>零件标准化对消费者有好处，造得更便宜，维护起来也更便宜。）

### 二、真正的动因：压力来自中国车厂

动因这一层几乎没人反驳。评论区里流传的判词很直接。

> "But this whole initiative is only happening because of competition, primarily from Chinese automakers!" — sebmellen [c:50596456]
> （译文：整个这项倡议之所以发生，只是因为竞争，主要是中国车厂带来的竞争！）

被点名的是垂直整合：BYD 的自产比例被说成 75%–85%，某些车型的外购件只剩车窗和轮胎。

> "They’re (the Japanese makers) are getting cooked by the Chinese makers like BYD who make 75%-85% of their parts in-house[1].<p>Depending on the car, some estimates say that only the windows and tires are the only pieces that come from outside suppliers.<p>[1] <a href=&quot;https:&#x2F;&#x2F;www.electrichybridvehicletechnology.com&#x2F;features&#x2F;feature-byd-taking-the-lead.html&quot; rel=&quot;nofollow&quot;>https:&#x2F;&#x2F;www.electrichybridvehicletechnology.com&#x2F;features&#x2F;fea...</a>" — wcfields [c:50596395]
> （译文：日本厂商正被 BYD 这样的中国厂商「煮熟」——它们有 75%–85% 的零件是自制的。<br>视车型而定，有估算说只有车窗和轮胎还来自外部供应商。<br>[1] https://www.electrichybridvehicletechnology.com/features/feature-byd-taking-the-lead.html）

这组数字出自评论者引用的第三方网站，本文未独立核实。另一条补充说，标准化对日本车厂本身也不算新事：Mazda 与 Subaru 已经在用大量 Toyota 的零件。

> "Mazda and Subaru already uses a lot of parts from Toyota, so they might be looped in my default. They are not considered one of the major Japanese automakers in terms of size." — etempleton [c:50596344]
> （译文：Mazda 和 Subaru 已经用了不少 Toyota 的零件，所以我猜它们默认也会被拉进来。按体量算，它们不算日本的主要汽车厂商。）

还有人不认为日本是被逼的：EV 比燃油车简单，混动只是中间状态，日本迟迟不做纯电，根源是产品定位保守，不是能力不行。

> "EVs are simpler than ICE, much less hybrids, and Japan has been infamously slow to build pure EVs.<p>So yes, but the drag is downstream of highly conservative product positioning not incompetence." — bpodgursky [c:50596458]
> （译文：EV 比燃油车简单，混动要更复杂一些，而日本造纯电的速度慢得出名。<br>所以是的，但拖后腿的是极度保守的产品定位，不是能力不行。）

### 三、标准化的另一面：趋同与停滞

反对者的火力集中在后果上：三家一起定标准，听起来像协作，实际更像把三家锁进同一份图纸。

> "Yeah, it&#x27;s not clear to me why people are just taking it as gospel that this will improve competitiveness. IMO it seems like it would lead to stagnation as it becomes more difficult for a single company to change anything." — modeless [c:50596524]
> （译文：我不明白为什么大家把「这会提升竞争力」当成不言自明。在我看来这会导致停滞，因为单家公司想改动任何东西都会变得更难。）

这条逻辑延伸出去是经济学式的反驳：单件定制的成本永远高于量产，这与用什么技术无关。

> "having a product with a quantity of 1 made for you is always going to be more expensive than making a million of a thing, this is economics independent of any technology<p>I&#x27;m not sure what general purpose robotics even is because the point of robotics is to <i>not be general purpose</i>, a general purpose robot is hardly faster than a general purpose human, the speed of the assembly line comes from recombining standardized parts, that&#x27;s true for human or robotic artisanal vs assembly production<p>the bespoke sandwich shop is always going to be more expensive than the sandwich factory even if the former is operated by a bot" — Barrin92 [c:50596755]
> （译文：为你一个人做一件产品，永远会比做一百万件同样的东西更贵，这是与技术无关的经济学。<br>我不太确定「通用机器人」到底指什么，因为机器人的意义本来就不是通用：通用机器人不会比通用的人快多少，流水线的速度来自对标准化零件的重新组合，人工匠作还是机器人匠作都一样，装配式生产永远更快。<br>手工三明治店永远比三明治工厂贵，哪怕前者由机器人经营。）

反方则指出，趋同这条趋势眼下只在软件以外成立：软件移植现在是「几美元加几分钟」，小众平台反而可能复活。

> "But this was because software was expensive. Actually now this could be the only field where the trend could be reversed. You could make any niche platform, and porting apps over would be a few dollars and some minutes." — poisonborz [c:50597229]
> （译文：但那是因为软件曾经很贵。其实现在这可能是唯一可能反转趋势的领域：你可以做任意一个小众平台，把应用移植过去只需要几美元和几分钟。）

还有人提醒，标准件不等于没差异化——标准件照样能拼出十几种设计。

> "And are all Android phones the same? You can have one with liquid cooling of battery and turbo fan cooler for CPU! Same will be with the car. You can make so many different designs with standard parts!" — ponector [c:50598021]
> （译文：难道所有 Android 手机都一样吗？有的带电池液冷，有的给 CPU 装涡轮风扇！车也一样。用标准件你照样能做出十几种不同设计！）

### 四、消费者要的是能互换的零件，不是能互换的标准

这一节是全场最实的一层：评论区用户抱怨的东西——灯泡、保险丝、电池端子——几乎都不在这次标准化的清单里。

> "I can think of at least 3 types of car battery terminal in-use today:  The old top-post, GM&#x27;s side-post, and Toyota&#x27;s (close-but-not-the-same!) top-post.  And that&#x27;s just the <i>terminals</i> -- nevermind the different sizes and mounting configurations.<p>I can screw in any of a whole galaxy of spark plugs into my Honda&#x27;s engine, but that doesn&#x27;t mean that the pistons won&#x27;t collide with them before reaching TDC.  (Does it matter if the screw threads are compatible when the parts crash before they can be used?)<p>Do we really have to talk about the light bulbs and the fuses, too?  It&#x27;s pretty boring, but I&#x27;ll be bored later if anyone really wants to get into that.<p>(I&#x27;d like some popcorn, first, if anyone feels like contributing something.)" — ssl-3 [c:50596482]
> （译文：现在在用的汽车电池端子至少就有 3 种：老式顶部端子、GM 的侧置端子，还有 Toyota 的顶部端子（看着像，其实不完全一样）。而这还只是<i>端子</i>——别提各种尺寸和安装方式了。<br>我能把一整银河系那么多种火花塞拧进我那台 Honda 的发动机，但这不意味着活塞不会在上止点之前撞上它们。（如果零件在能用之前就撞碎了，螺纹兼容又有意义吗？）<br>我们是不是还得聊聊灯泡和保险丝？那话题挺无聊的，不过等会儿我无聊了欢迎继续。<br>（先来点爆米花吧，谁想接着聊就聊。））

共享零件还有质量这一层顾虑，有人直接给 Ford 与 Chevy 共用的变速箱下了判词。

> "And it has been a complete disaster. Half of them are junk from mile one and the rest are ticking time bombs." — camcil [c:50596452]
> （译文：结果是一场彻底的灾难。其中一半从第一英里起就是垃圾，剩下的全是定时炸弹。）

而来自修车一线的观察把「标准」和「货架」这两件事连了起来：标准是有的，但零件型号太多，店家不值得占货架。

> "Headlights have gone the other direction in my lifetime, to the point where I see more hazed and cracked lenses than I see good ones - bespoke replacements are too expensive to bother with.<p>Was chatting with the local hardware store owner a few weeks back about bulbs and solenoids - there are standards, but the breadth of different types in cars on the road means it isn&#x27;t worth the shelf space to stock them - individual types move slowly while stocking enough variety to cover what the local market needs takes space away from more profitable things (like metric-thread fasteners).<p>Even nearby auto parts store end up ordering common parts from regional warehouses, putting them on roughly equal footing with RockAuto in terms of time to delivery (and of course at a disadvantage on price).<p>And this doesn&#x27;t even get into truly unique parts like door latch motors or the various ICs that control various subsystems.<p>Frankly, it&#x27;s a mess. Having a spare &quot;parts car&quot; sitting around is best if you have the space for it." — Shog9 [c:50596528]
> （译文：我这辈子眼看着车灯反其道而行，现在路上看到的雾化和开裂灯罩比完好的还多，单独定做的替换件太贵，没人折腾。<br>几周前我跟本地五金店老板聊灯泡和电磁阀：标准是有的，但路上跑的车型差异太大，不值得为此占货架——单一型号走得慢，要备齐本地市场需要的种类，占掉的就是更赚钱的东西（比如公制螺纹紧固件）的位置。<br>连旁边的汽车配件店也只能从区域仓库订常用件，在到货时间上跟 RockAuto 大致持平（价格上当然吃亏）。<br>这还没算上门锁电机、各类控制子系统的 IC 这些真正独一份的零件。<br>说白了，就是一团乱。有地方放的话，养一辆备件车是最靠谱的。）

### 五、竞争到底对消费者好不好：他们本可以更早合作

这一节把「竞争让价格更低」这句常见话拆开来看，而拆开的方向对车厂不太有利。

> "I&#x27;m fascinated you think the market will produce a best product and then stop. Most of what the market does is figure out how to sell people a shittier product at a higher price." — serioussecurity [c:50596500]
> （译文：你们以为市场会产出一个最好的产品然后就此打住，这想法我很感兴趣。市场大部分时间在做的事，是想办法把更差的产品卖得更贵。）

于是有人把这条新闻当成对「竞争对消费者有好处」这句话的反证。

> "Think of this the next time someone tells you competition is good for consumers… sounds they could have brought down prices at any time, if they’d cooperated, hey?" — kennywinker [c:50596372]
> （译文：下次有人跟你说竞争对消费者有好处的时候想想这条——听起来他们随时都可以合作把价格压下来，不是吗？）

回应的一方指出，这份倡议本身就是竞争逼出来的，锅不该甩给「竞争不够」。再往下就变成了另一种主张：用立法强制标准化，把价格压下去。这条路上还牵出一串货架经济学。

> "Manufactures like SC Johnson (Windex maker) pay major retailers for shelf space. The more variety, the less shelf space a competitor has.<p>That’s why there’s 100 different toothpastes that all effectively are the same." — wcfields [c:50596364]
> （译文：像 SC Johnson（Windex 的厂商）这样的制造商会付钱给大零售商买货架。品类越多，竞争对手能分到的货架就越少。<br>所以才有 100 种功效其实都一样的牙膏。）

评论区把这条逻辑一路延伸到超市：罐装豆子、肉、奶酪、蔬菜，统一种类只挂自家牌子；Trader Joe's 就是 Aldi 系的模式。牙膏、肥皂、洗洁精、谷物，全是同一个套利结构。

### 六、没人想要的那部分：联网、遥测与信息娱乐

省成本的方向，评论区不约而同地指向了车里那块没人要求过的电子模块。

> "Perhaps the next cost saving measure should be to remove the cellular modem that nobody asked for and that shamelessly spies on drivers. Unless, of course, Toyota et al. do in fact get a kickback from dealers, insurance companies, and data brokers for invading their customers&#x27; privacy. In some of the cars these companies make, you need to either pull a fuse (best case) or take the dash apart to unplug the unwanted &quot;bug.&quot;" — drnick1 [c:50596362]
> （译文：也许下一个该采取的降本措施，是把那个没人要求过、还厚颜无耻地监视驾驶员的蜂窝通信模块拆掉。当然，除非 Toyota 这些公司真的能从经销商、保险公司和数据贩子那里拿到回扣，换取侵犯客户隐私的权利。在它们造的一些车上，你得拔保险丝（最好的情况），或者干脆拆开中控台，才能把那个不受欢迎的「虫」 unplug 掉。）

有人指出中国车厂也在做同样的事，所以省这块成本未必帮得上利润——除非市场上真的出现一批不带间谍硬件的车。

> "I think Chinese car manufacturers are doing this as well, so while I personally would never choose a car with that stuff in it unless I was certain I could disable it, I don’t agree that this would help their bottom line. Not until there’s a significant market for cars that don’t have spy tech." — left-struck [c:50596620]
> （译文：我觉得中国车厂也在做同样的事，所以我自己绝不会选一台带这种玩意的车，除非我确定能把它关掉；但我不同意这会帮到他们的利润。除非市场上出现一批相当规模的不带间谍技术的车。）

信息娱乐系统被单独拎出来要求统一，得到的回答是：技术上不是不可能，但没有任何一方有动机。

> "The infotainment system gets at least an on&#x2F;off signal from the car. That signal is a number that differs from brand to brand and even from model to model. So standardizing the infotainment also means standardization of the communication signals (at least some of them). Not impossible, but there are no incentives." — GoToRO [c:50596877]
> （译文：信息娱乐系统至少要从车上接收一个开关信号。这个信号是一个数值，各品牌不同，甚至各型号都不同。所以统一信息娱乐系统，也意味着统一（至少一部分）通信信号。不是做不到，但没有任何动机。）

法规那边还有一道墙：欧洲已经强制要求车具备紧急呼叫能力，硬件本来就得在。

> "Never going to happen. Europe already requires emergency calling from your car so the hardware already needs to be there. <a href=&quot;https:&#x2F;&#x2F;europa.eu&#x2F;youreurope&#x2F;citizens&#x2F;travel&#x2F;security-and-emergencies&#x2F;emergency-assistance-vehicles-ecall&#x2F;indexamp_en.htm&quot; rel=&quot;nofollow&quot;>https:&#x2F;&#x2F;europa.eu&#x2F;youreurope&#x2F;citizens&#x2F;travel&#x2F;security-and-em...</a>" — lmz [c:50597024]
> （译文：永远不可能发生。欧洲已经要求车辆具备紧急呼叫功能，所以这块硬件本来就必须在车里。<br>https://europa.eu/youreurope/citizens/travel/security-and-emergencies/emergency-assistance-vehicles-ecall/indexamp_en.htm）

### 七、日本为什么不全电：自食其利还是等着被吃

最后一节把标准化放回更大的背景：七家日本车厂之所以同时动标准件，是因为电动化路线上各有各的难处，而最难看的那条是自相残杀。

> "Subjectively, I think Japan was smart to not go full on EV, given the constraints they have; charging infrastructure, battery sourcing, and range.  At the time when Tesla was selling under 500k cars (2020 and earlier) a year, there wasn&#x27;t a problem with battery supply (they were able to ramp up). ## 总体情绪

这个帖子表面在讨论工业标准，底下争的是三件事：标准化的收益归谁、成本由谁承担、以及消费者到底需不需要差异。

支持方的底气来自可核对的东西：三家主机厂确实坐在同一张谈判桌旁，先从钢材、塑料和线束色标这三块不起眼的部分做起，明年就落地。质疑方的底气来自另一侧的常识：市场不会主动收敛到最优，标准存在也不等于同一件东西，而标准化往往意味着单家公司更难改动什么。两边都没能压过对方——因为反对标准化的人里，有相当一部分自己也在抱怨零件号不统一。

最能说明问题的其实是两份清单的错位：被统一的钢、塑料和线束色标，消费者一件也感觉不到；消费者天天在骂的灯泡、保险丝、电池端子、蜂窝模块、零件号，没有一项在这三块里。附带的那条处方倒很对症——省下来的钱如果继续填进每几年一次的改款和那台没人要求过的信息娱乐机，账面上的成本下降很快会被别处吃回去。

他们统一了钢、塑料和线束的颜色，唯独没统一那些让人多花钱的东西。
